Bush Barrow

In 1798 the father of excavation William Cunnington reviewed excavations in Wiltshire. Cunnington's work was founded by patrons who's money helped him further his investigations. His recordings of neolithic and Bronze Age barrows are still highly vauled by archaeologist today, His terms and definitions shaped the conversations around archaeology, for example the use of a trowel was first revealed in a letter from Cunnington to one of his patrons in 1808 describing how John Parker used one in the excavation of Bush Barrow. One major achievements of 19th century archaeology was stratigraphy. This helped place a timeline on artifacts.
